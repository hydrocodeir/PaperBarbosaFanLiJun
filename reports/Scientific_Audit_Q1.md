# Scientific audit of the Q1 manuscript

## Executive assessment

The manuscript is scientifically defensible for an observational, station-network study of recent thermal-extreme and compound dry–hot changes. It is suitable for journal submission after completion of the data-provenance and author-declaration templates and a clean rerun from the declared environment.

## Strengths

1. The objectives, estimands, data period, and station domain are explicit.
2. Quantile-specific slopes are reported with station-level uncertainty and FDR-controlled median inference.
3. The manuscript separates network summaries from station-level inference and distinguishes station extent from physical area.
4. Fixed-baseline, homogeneity-exclusion, bootstrap-method, bootstrap-depth, internal-consistency, and residual-block sensitivity checks address different uncertainty sources.
5. Compound-event claims are restricted to empirical within-record rarity classes and include dependence-aware trend checks.
6. The supplied reference papers are used as methodological and rhetorical benchmarks without copying their text.
7. Eleven main figures now cover distributional trends, spatial structure, climate regimes, temporal robustness, and compound-event extent, frequency, composition, and connectedness; data-quality diagnostics remain available as supplementary output.

## Major issues still requiring attention before submission

- Replace the data-source, access, license, repository, and archive placeholders with verified statements.
- Confirm the final author contributions, funding, competing interests, acknowledgements, and permanent code/data identifiers.
- Execute a clean end-to-end rerun from the pinned environment and compare generated tables and figures with the reported values.
- If the target journal requires simultaneous field inference or formal attribution, add those analyses or retain the current explicitly limited scope.

## Statistical and interpretive safeguards

- The 10- and 20-year compound thresholds are empirical rarity classes, not stable long-return-period hazard estimates.
- Quantiles refer to annual event-count distributions, not daily intensity quantiles.
- Climate-regime and geographic differences are descriptive associations; no causal driver is identified.
- Station counts are not area-weighted and should not be described as affected land area.
- Cluster assignments are exploratory because stability is uneven across indices.

## Validation status

- Input integrity checks: PASS (1,539,956 rows; 124 stations; no duplicate station-days; station IDs matched).
- Numerical consistency checks against existing CSV outputs: PASS for the principal values reported in the manuscript.
- Python syntax compilation: PASS.
- Clean full-pipeline rerun in the current session: NOT RUN; existing outputs were generated by prior recorded runs and should be regenerated once before submission.

## Final recommendation

Major revision is complete at the manuscript level, but the paper should not be labeled submission-ready until provenance placeholders are resolved and the clean rerun is archived.
